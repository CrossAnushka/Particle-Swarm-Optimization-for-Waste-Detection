\begin{abstract}
Automatic waste sorting requires detectors that can locate every item in an image, but a large part of the existing work still classifies single objects photographed against plain backgrounds. Moreover, studies that tune such models with metaheuristics seldom include a random-search baseline or report confidence intervals. In this paper, we examine whether particle swarm optimization (PSO) yields a better lightweight waste detector than random search or the library default settings when all three are given the same budget. Six training hyperparameters of YOLOv8n are selected in three ways: the defaults, the best of 32 random samples, and a swarm of 8 particles run for 4 rounds. Both searches use 32 short proxy trainings, and the proxy was verified beforehand against longer training (Spearman correlation 0.976, first generation). Each selected setting is then trained fully and tested once on held-out data, and the differences are assessed with paired-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals. The experiment was carried out in two generations: the first uses only studio images, while the second adds real-world photographs and an Indian street set that is kept for testing only. On studio data the three methods are tied, with a mean average precision at an intersection-over-union of 0.5 (\mapfifty) of 0.676, 0.672 and 0.668 for the defaults, random search and PSO, respectively. When real-world training data are added, both searches outperform the defaults significantly on the mixed test set (by 0.017 and 0.020 \mapfifty), but they do not differ from each other. On real-world photographs (0.298--0.310) no difference is significant, and on the Indian photographs all models reach only 0.023--0.027. On identical test images, adding real-world training data improved real-world \mapfifty from 0.038--0.096 to 0.338--0.353 and reduced studio \mapfifty by 0.074--0.105, all significantly. These results show that the choice of optimizer has little effect in comparison with the training data, and that tuning claims should be supported by an equal-budget random-search baseline and confidence intervals.
\end{abstract}
